POST-READ NOTES · EMBEDDINGS: MEANING AS GEOMETRY

Some questions have no clean filter. "Something about my bill looks wrong" names no
field, no date, no category — it names a meaning. Embeddings are the tool for that
residue. An embedding model maps a piece of text to a list of numbers — a vector —
such that texts with similar meaning land near each other. That is the entire idea:
meaning becomes geometry, and "find related text" becomes "find nearby points".

The vectors are unglamorous. A small local model produces 384 floats per text; a large
hosted one produces 3,072. More dimensions give the space more ways to distinguish
meanings, but the object is the same list of numbers, and you can print it.

Similarity is measured by the angle between vectors: cosine similarity, 1.0 for
identical directions, near 0 for unrelated ones. If you normalize every vector to
length one first, the cosine collapses into a plain dot product — one multiply-add per
dimension. Searching a corpus is then a single matrix-vector multiplication: score
every chunk, sort, take the top k. For a corpus of hundreds or thousands of chunks,
that matrix sitting in memory IS your vector database. The managed products earn their
place when the matrix outgrows RAM or needs many concurrent writers — at that boundary,
not before.
